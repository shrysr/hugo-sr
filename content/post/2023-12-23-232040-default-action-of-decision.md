CassieKozyrkov provides a number of pointers[^fn:1] to improve the making of a decision.

There are a number of effects that can prove detrimental to the goal of making a good or the right decision, even when one is data driven. For example the IkeaEffect, ConfirmationBias and others.

One way of countering the effects of the above is to decide a default decision in advance. "What am I going to do if I have to make the decision right now?". This is based on the data that is available right now, and is essentially a choice of the 'least evil'.

So, say the answer is no: it does not stop with the default decision. Once it is clear, the next step is to think about resolution of the above, in the sense, what would be required to revert the answer to a Yes?

Suppose the answer is Yes, one could then consider a reversed thinking of what would make the answer 'No' - as a way of identifying and preferably mitigating risks. This would be aking to the approach of using different points of view to think,[^fn:2] in the sense of generating ideas, questions and comments from different view points.

In short, the default decision will help open up a pathway of thought experiments that can lead you to the ideal set of next steps to be taken. The ideal set of steps should also consider other important factors like the time and resources available, risk appetite and so on.

The key here lies in framing of the question answered by the default decision. This means squelching the entire effort and objective into a single, effective question that is entirely relevant and pivotal. This would be possible only through an intimate understanding of the effort or project and the question or problem that is being resolved.

While the above may seem almost distasteful for a passionate person working on a solution - in a larger context, and from a business context - it makes a great deal of sense. Not all are paid to wonder down the rabbit hole with the hope of finding some treasure in the end. Most projects beyond a certain size and scope can seldom be executed alone - and when additional resources are asked, there has to be a justification, and a reasoning behind the purpose of the whole project.
